People often ask whether they should get “air conditioning” or a “heat pump”. For the systems fitted in many homes, those labels overlap more than the names suggest.

## Cooling-only air conditioning

A cooling-only split system moves heat **out** of the room in summer. It does not heat in winter. These are less common in new domestic installs in the UK, because most manufacturers’ wall-mounted and multi-split ranges now offer reverse-cycle models as standard.

## Air-to-air heat pumps (reverse-cycle)

An air-to-air heat pump is a split (or multi-split) system that can run in both directions:

- **Cooling mode** — heat is pumped outdoors (same idea as traditional AC)
- **Heating mode** — heat is drawn from outdoor air and delivered indoors

The indoor units look like familiar wall-mounted or cassette heads. The outdoor unit still sits outside. What changes is the refrigerant cycle and the controls: you get comfort year-round from the same kit.

That is why installers often describe these as air conditioning **and** heating, or as air-to-air heat pumps — they are the same family of equipment.

## How this differs from an air-to-water heat pump

Air-to-water heat pumps warm radiators or underfloor heating and often provide hot water. They are a different product: different outdoor unit, different pipework, different design process.

Air-to-air systems heat (and cool) the air in the room directly. They do not replace your boiler’s hot-water cylinder on their own. Many homes use air-to-air for room comfort alongside an existing heating system.

## What that means if you are choosing kit

Ask what you need the system to do:

- **Summer cooling only** — a cooling-focused split may be enough, though reverse-cycle is usually the better long-term choice
- **Cooling plus useful winter heating** — an air-to-air heat pump (reverse-cycle split or multi-split) is the usual answer
- **Whole-house radiator or underfloor heating** — that is air-to-water territory, not a wall-mounted AC conversation

Efficiency, noise, outdoor placement and room sizing still matter as much as the label on the brochure.

## Cambridge homes in practice

Loft bedrooms and south-facing rooms often need cooling first. Once people see that the same outdoor unit can take the chill off a cold morning, reverse-cycle becomes the default recommendation.
