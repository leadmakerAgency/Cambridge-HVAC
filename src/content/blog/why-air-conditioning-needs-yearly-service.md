Air conditioning is easy to ignore when it still switches on and cools the room. That is exactly when a yearly service earns its place.

Dust, pollen and household debris build up on filters and coils. Drainage lines clog. Joints can weep refrigerant slowly enough that you only notice when the room takes longer to cool.

## What a service usually covers

A proper service is more than wiping the front grille. Typical checks include:

- Cleaning or replacing filters
- Checking indoor and outdoor coils
- Testing airflow and temperatures
- Inspecting the condensate drain for blockages
- Checking electrical connections and running pressures where appropriate
- Looking for early signs of wear or refrigerant loss

F-Gas rules also mean refrigerant handling is not DIY. If a system needs refrigerant work, it has to be done by a certified engineer.

## Why “it still works” is not enough

A dirty filter makes the fan work harder and the room feel stuffy. A partially blocked drain can drip into walls or ceilings. Low refrigerant does not always mean a dramatic failure — often it means higher bills and a unit that never quite reaches the set temperature.

Catching those issues once a year is cheaper and less disruptive than an emergency call on the first hot weekend of the year.

## When to book

Late spring is ideal for many Cambridge homes: the system is checked before summer demand, and any parts or repairs can be planned without a heatwave deadline.

If the unit has been idle all winter, a service before you rely on it is especially useful. Heat-pump systems that heat in winter and cool in summer benefit from the same routine attention.

## Between services

You can keep the system healthier between visits:

- Clean or vacuum washable filters as the manufacturer recommends
- Keep outdoor units clear of leaves, ivy and stored clutter
- Leave indoor units with clear airflow — not blocked by curtains or furniture
- Note unusual noises, smells or weak cooling and mention them at the service
