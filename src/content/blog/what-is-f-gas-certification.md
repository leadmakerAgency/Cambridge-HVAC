If you ask enough questions before booking an air conditioning installation, F-Gas certification comes up quickly. It is not a marketing badge. It is a legal requirement, and it is the single most useful thing to check before letting anyone touch a refrigerant system in your home.

## What "F-Gas" actually refers to

Most air conditioning and heat pump systems use fluorinated gases (F-gases) as refrigerant. These gases are effective at moving heat, but they are also potent greenhouse gases if released into the atmosphere, which is why their use is tightly regulated in both installation and disposal.

## Why certification is a legal requirement

By law, anyone who installs, services, maintains or decommissions equipment containing F-gas refrigerants has to hold the relevant certification. It is not optional, and it is not just for large commercial systems, it applies to the wall-mounted unit going into a bedroom as much as it applies to a shop's air conditioning.

The official guidance on this sits on [gov.uk's page on F-Gas certification](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/certification-for-companies-working-on-equipment-containing-f-gas), which sets out exactly who needs to be certified and what for.

## REFCOM and the register

In the UK, [REFCOM](https://www.refcom.org.uk/) is the largest F-Gas certification scheme, appointed to certify companies working with refrigerants. Once a company is certified, it appears on REFCOM's public register, which anyone can search by company name or registration number before booking work. Certificates are not indefinite either, they run for three years and have to be renewed, so a live registration actually means something.

## What this means in practice

A few things follow directly from this:

- **It protects your warranty.** Most manufacturer warranties require the system to be installed and serviced by a certified engineer. Uncertified work can void it outright.
- **It is about doing the job safely.** Refrigerant handling done badly is a genuine safety and environmental issue, not just paperwork.
- **It is easy to check.** A legitimate installer will tell you their certification body and registration number without hesitation, and you can verify it yourself on the register.

## The one question to ask

Before booking anyone to install or service an air conditioning system, ask which F-Gas certification scheme they are registered with, and check the register yourself. It takes two minutes and tells you more than a portfolio of photos ever will.
