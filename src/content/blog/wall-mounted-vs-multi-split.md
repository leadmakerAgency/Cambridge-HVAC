Choosing air conditioning often starts with a simple question: do you want to cool one room properly, or several rooms from one outdoor unit?

## Wall-mounted (single-split)

A wall-mounted system pairs one indoor unit with one outdoor unit. It is the most common setup for a bedroom, living room or home office.

It suits you if:

- One space gets uncomfortably hot and the rest of the house is fine
- Outdoor space for a condenser is limited
- You want the simplest install and the fewest decisions

The indoor unit sits high on the wall. A short refrigerant pipe run connects it to the outdoor unit, usually on an exterior wall, balcony or flat roof.

## Multi-split

A multi-split system uses **one outdoor unit** and **two or more indoor units**. Each indoor unit can usually be controlled separately, so bedrooms and living spaces can run at different temperatures.

It suits you if:

- Several rooms need cooling (or heating with a heat-pump model)
- You would rather not put a separate outdoor unit on every wall that needs cooling
- You want room-by-room control without a ducted system

The outdoor unit has to be sized for the combined load. That is why a survey matters: undersized outdoor units struggle on hot days; oversized ones cycle inefficiently.

## Which fits a Cambridge home?

Terraces and semis often start with a single wall-mounted unit in the hottest bedroom or loft conversion. Flats with one main living space do the same.

Larger houses, or homes where upstairs bedrooms and a downstairs sitting room all overheat, lean toward multi-split — one tidy outdoor unit, several indoor heads.

There is no universal “best”. The right answer depends on which rooms you use in summer, how much outdoor space you have, and how the pipe runs would look through the fabric of the building.
