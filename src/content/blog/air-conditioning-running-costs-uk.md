"How much will it cost to run?" is a fair question, and an honest answer starts with what actually determines the number, rather than a single figure that will not match your home anyway.

## What actually drives the cost

Running cost comes down to three things multiplying together: how efficient the unit is, how many hours it runs, and what you pay per unit of electricity.

- **Efficiency** — modern inverter systems ramp up and down rather than switching fully on and off, so they use noticeably less power to hold a temperature than older on/off units.
- **Hours of use** — a system cooling one bedroom overnight costs a fraction of one running across a whole house all day.
- **Electricity price** — this is the variable people forget. Ofgem's price cap sets the ceiling on typical unit rates for households on standard tariffs, and it changes every few months, so a running-cost figure quoted last year is already out of date.

You can see the current rate for yourself on [Ofgem's energy price cap page](https://www.ofgem.gov.uk/energy-regulation/domestic-and-non-domestic/energy-pricing-rules/energy-price-cap), which is the actual source, rather than a number recycled from an old blog post.

## Why UK usage patterns matter more than the spec sheet

A system's efficiency rating is measured under standard test conditions, not your loft bedroom on the one genuinely hot week of the year. In practice, UK air conditioning tends to run hard for short, intense bursts rather than continuously through a mild season, which is a very different load pattern to somewhere with a long, hot summer. That is part of why a system sized and set up properly for your rooms matters more to the real-world bill than the number on the box.

## The heating side of the equation

Because most of the systems we install are reverse-cycle, the same unit can provide efficient heating in the cooler months too. Energy Saving Trust's [guide to air-to-air heat pumps](https://energysavingtrust.org.uk/advice/air-to-air-heat-pumps/) is a good independent explainer of how that side of things works, and why a well-specified system can end up earning its keep across the year rather than sitting idle for nine months of it.

## Practical ways to keep the running cost down

- Set a sensible target temperature rather than the coldest setting, every extra degree of difference costs more
- Use timers or scheduling so the system is not cooling an empty house
- Keep filters clean, a clogged filter makes the system work harder for the same result
- Close curtains and blinds on the hottest side of the house during the day to reduce the load in the first place
