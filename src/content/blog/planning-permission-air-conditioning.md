It is one of the first questions people ask before an installation: will I need planning permission for this? For most houses, the answer is no, but "most" is doing some work in that sentence, and the exceptions are worth knowing before you settle on where the outdoor unit goes.

## The short answer

Adding air conditioning to a house is usually covered by permitted development, which means you do not need to apply for planning permission, provided the installation meets a set of limits and conditions set out in legislation. Those limits cover the size of the outdoor unit, how many you can have, and where it can be sited.

## Where it gets specific

A few conditions catch people out:

- **Size** — for a house, the outdoor unit (including its housing) generally needs to stay under 1.5 cubic metres. Flats have a tighter limit.
- **Number of units** — detached houses can usually have two outdoor units under permitted development; semi-detached and terraced houses are limited to one.
- **Roof position** — a unit cannot go on a pitched roof, and if it sits on a flat roof it needs to be at least a metre from the edge.
- **Heating function matters** — permitted development rights for this kind of equipment generally apply where the system provides heating (including systems that heat and cool). A cooling-only system does not qualify in the same way, which is one more reason reverse-cycle systems have become the default recommendation.
- **Noise** — the unit needs to meet a noise standard assessed at your nearest neighbour's closest window, so position and unit choice both matter.

## Conservation areas and listed buildings

If your home sits in a conservation area, you generally keep permitted development rights, but with an extra condition: the unit usually cannot go on a wall or roof facing a road. Listed buildings are a different matter entirely, and will usually need listed building consent regardless of size.

Cambridge has a fair number of both, so this is exactly the kind of thing we check before we ever talk about where a unit is going to sit.

## Where the official guidance lives

We are not going to try to summarise every edge case here, because the rules do get updated. The definitive source is the [Planning Portal's guidance on air conditioning](https://interactive.planningportal.co.uk/faqs/do-i-need-planning-permission-to-add-air-conditioning-to-my-home), and for anything to do with heat pump equipment specifically, their [heat pump planning permission guidance](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/permission/common-projects/heat-pumps/planning-permission-air-source-heat-pump/) covers the same ground from that angle.
